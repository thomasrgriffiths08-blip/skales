What should a website for padel and tennis clubs do?

A padel or tennis club’s website needs the courts, opening times and prices on the page, and a way for players to book a court and pay online without ringing the club. It should also sell coaching, junior courses and taster sessions in the same place, remind players before they play, and ask new players for a review once they have been on court.

  1. 01The courts, surfaces, opening hours and floodlit times on the page, with prices for members and for pay and play.
  2. 02Court booking and payment online, so a slot is only taken once it is paid.
  3. 03Taster sessions and beginner courses for people who have never played, bookable on the same page.
  4. 04Coaching, junior courses and holiday camps with their dates, levels and places left.
  5. 05A plain route into membership for players who keep coming back.

Padel and tennis clubs: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a padel or tennis club?

A new player usually finds a club by searching for courts near them, then checks the price, the surface and whether they can just turn up and play. Regulars want to book their usual slot quickly. The leaks are courts held by a message and never paid for, beginners who do not know where to start, and coaching courses that fill by word of mouth rather than from the website.

Can players book and pay for a court online?

Yes. They pick a court, a date and a time, and pay in the same booking, so the court is only taken once it is paid.

Can I sell coaching and junior courses on the same site?

Yes. Courses and camps sit alongside court booking, each with its dates, level and the places left.

Can beginners book a taster session?

Yes. A taster or beginner session is bookable on its own, so a new player can start without ringing the club.
