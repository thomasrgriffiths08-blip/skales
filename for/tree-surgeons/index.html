What it needsTree surgeons

What should a website for tree surgeons do?

  • Your NPTC certificates and public liability insurance shown plainly, not hidden on an About page.
  • A page for each job: felling, crown reduction, pruning, hedge work and stump grinding.
  • How you check for Tree Preservation Orders and conservation areas, and who makes the application.
  • What happens to the wood and chip, whether you take it away or leave logs, said up front.
  • An emergency number for storm damage, kept separate from the form for routine quotes.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a tree surgeon?

People know tree work is dangerous, so they look for proof before they ring: qualifications, insurance, photos of jobs close to houses and roads. Jobs are priced from a site visit and customers often get more than one quote, so quotes go quiet and need chasing. Storms bring urgent calls about fallen limbs and split trunks, and every one of them arrives while you are up a tree.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Tree surgeons: questions people ask

Should my website explain Tree Preservation Orders?

A short page on how you check whether a tree is protected, and how you handle the application, answers a worry plenty of customers have. Keep it to what you do and link to the council for the rules themselves.

Can I send the quote straight after the site visit?

Yes. You price the job on your phone and send it from the CRM, and the customer gets a link to read and accept it. If it goes quiet, a follow-up goes out a couple of days later.

Will a website help with storm call-outs?

It helps people find you and reach you. The emergency number sits at the top of every page, and every call you miss gets a text back, so the people who rang during the storm are not lost while you work.
