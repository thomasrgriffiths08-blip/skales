What should a website for kitchen and bathroom fitters do?

A website for a kitchen or bathroom fitter is mostly a gallery that sells: finished rooms photographed properly, grouped by style, with a clear line on whether you supply and fit or fit only. It should let customers book a design visit online and follow up the quote afterwards, because these jobs are compared and decided over weeks.

  1. 01A gallery of finished kitchens and bathrooms, grouped by style, with close-ups of the joins, edges and worktops.
  2. 02A plain answer to the first question: do you supply and fit, fit only, or both.
  3. 03Design visits bookable online, with the option of a deposit that comes off the job.
  4. 04The steps from measure to handover, so customers know who does what and when.

Questions

Kitchen and bathroom fitters: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a kitchen fitter?

Customers start with pictures, saving rooms they like long before they ring anyone. Plenty visit a big showroom as well as a local fitter, so the fitter has to show work at least as good and explain the difference: one team, start to finish. The leaks are design visits that never become a quote, and quotes that sit unanswered while the customer weighs it up.

Can customers get a rough price before I visit?

Yes, if you want them to. A quote tool built on your own prices can show a guide figure as they choose units and worktops, and the design visit firms it up.

Should kitchens and bathrooms have separate pages?

Yes. They are different searches and often different customers, and each page can answer its own questions: worktops and appliances on one, wet rooms and accessible bathrooms on the other.

Can design visits be booked online?

Yes. Customers pick a free slot from your diary, add a note about the room and pay a deposit if you take one. The visit lands in your diary with their details and what they are after.
