From first enquiry to fitted, without a quote going cold.

A window and door fitter’s website needs the products you fit, from windows and composite doors to bifolds and repairs, photos of your own installations, and a way to book a survey or ask for a price online. What should a website for window and door fitters do?

  • The products you fit, such as windows, doors, bifolds and roof lanterns, with the materials and colours you offer.
  • Photos of your own installations, inside and out, rather than pictures from a manufacturer’s brochure.
  • Surveys bookable online, with a short form about the property and how many windows or doors need replacing.
  • A separate route for repairs, such as misted units, locks and handles, so small jobs are not lost among the quotes.
  • Any scheme or trade body you are registered with, your insurance and the guarantee you give, stated plainly.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a window fitter?

Replacement windows are a big decision, so customers usually ask a few companies for a survey, compare the quotes at the kitchen table and take their time. Enquiries come in at evenings and weekends, surveys have to fit around your installations, and after the quote there is often silence. Once they say yes there is a wait while the frames are made, and that is when customers ring to ask what is happening. Repairs, such as misted glass and broken handles, come in all year and are easy to miss when you are on a fit.

Window and door fitters: questions people ask

Can customers book a window survey online?

Yes. They choose a time from the survey slots you open, tell you about the property and how many windows or doors they have in mind, and get a confirmation with what to expect on the visit.

How do I stop customers chasing me about fitting dates?

Keep them updated before they need to ask. Each job has its stage on your board, and a message goes when the order is placed, when the fitting date is set and the day before you arrive.

Should repairs have their own page?

Yes. People searching for a misted unit or a broken handle want a quick fix, not a sales visit. A separate page with its own form brings those jobs in, and a good repair gives them a reason to call you first when the windows need replacing.
