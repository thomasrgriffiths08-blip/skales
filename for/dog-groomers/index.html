What it needsDog groomers

What should a website for dog groomers do?

  • Booking that asks for breed, size and coat, so a full groom and a nail trim do not get the same slot.
  • A first-visit form for the dog’s age, health, vet and any behaviour notes, filled in before the day.
  • Your services set out plainly: full grooms, bath and tidy, puppy introductions, nails and hand-stripping.
  • Photos of dogs you have groomed, by breed, with their owners’ permission.
  • Where you are, or the area your van covers, and how drop-off and collection work.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a dog groomer?

Owners pick a groomer they trust with their dog, usually on a recommendation or from photos of dogs like theirs, and then they tend to stay. The work comes round every few weeks for most coats, but it leaks when rebooking is left to the owner, when a new customer’s questions go unanswered while you have a dog on the table, and when a late cancellation leaves a long slot empty.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Dog groomers: questions people ask

Can owners book a dog groom online?

Yes. They choose the service and give the breed, size and coat, and the booking only offers slots long enough for that dog. New customers fill in the first-visit form at the same time, so you know the dog before it arrives.

Can regulars be rebooked automatically?

Yes. When a groom is finished, the next one is offered at the interval you set for that dog, and if the owner has not booked, a reminder goes out with a link to the next free slot.

Does it work for a mobile grooming van?

Yes. The website lists the postcodes you cover, the booking asks for the address, and you can open slots by area on the days you are in each one, so the van is not crossing town between dogs.
