What it needsPhysiotherapists and clinics

What should a website for physiotherapists and clinics do?

  • Each physio’s HCPC registration number, and CSP membership if they have it.
  • Pages for the problems people search for: back pain, neck pain, sports injuries, knee and shoulder problems.
  • Online booking by appointment type and clinician, with first assessments longer than follow-ups.
  • Whether you see insured patients, and what they need to have ready.
  • Parking, access and what to wear, so the first visit holds no surprises.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a physio?

Patients search for their symptom before they search for a physio, and they want to know quickly whether you treat it, whether they need a referral and whether you see insured patients. They book a first assessment, often online and out of hours. Work leaks after that: follow-ups not booked once the pain eases, late cancellations, and calls that ring out while you are hands-on with someone.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Physiotherapists and clinics: questions people ask

Should my website say patients can self-refer?

Yes, plainly and near the top. People who have never seen a physio often assume they need their GP first, and it is one of the first things they check.

Can patients book a specific physio?

Yes. They choose the appointment type and the clinician, then a time that clinician has free. A first assessment can be set to take longer than a follow-up.

Can the booking form handle insurance patients?

Yes. It can ask whether they are claiming through insurance and for their authorisation details, so you have them before the first session.
