What it needs

What should a website for architects do?

An architect’s website needs projects shown with plans and drawings as well as finished photographs, a plain explanation of how you work from first visit to completion, and your ARB registration stated clearly. It should also let homeowners book a first consultation or site visit online, and follow up the fee proposals that go quiet.

  1. 01Projects shown with sketches, plans and sections as well as finished photographs.
  2. 02How you work, stage by stage, including planning and building regulations, in plain words.
  3. 03Your ARB registration, and RIBA chartered practice status if you have it.
  4. 04The kinds of project you take on, from extensions to new builds, and where.
  5. 05An enquiry form that asks about the site, the brief and the timescale before the first call.

Questions

Architects: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose an architect?

Clients arrive through recommendations, local searches and photos of work they admire, usually with a project in mind and little idea how the process works. They want to see projects like theirs, understand the stages and know what happens when, before they commit to a fee. Work leaks when a site visit leads to a fee proposal that nobody follows up, and when enquiries with no site or no budget take as long as the serious ones.

I’m not ARB registered. What should my site call me?

Not an architect: in the UK the title is protected, and only people on the ARB register can use it. Architectural designer or architectural technologist describe the work honestly, and the site can make the most of either.

Should I show drawings or only finished photos?

Both. Finished photos show the result, but plans and sketches show how you think, which is what a client is really hiring. They also make your site look like nobody else’s.

Is it worth chasing fee proposals?

Yes. A proposal that goes quiet can be waiting on a conversation at home rather than a no, and one polite follow-up a few days later reminds them you are ready when they are.
