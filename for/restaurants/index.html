What it needs

What should a website for restaurants and bars do?

A restaurant or bar website needs table booking on your own site, a menu written as text with dietary and allergen information, and opening hours that match Google. It should take deposits for large groups and the nights that sell out, and handle private dining and party enquiries separately from everyday tables.

  1. 01Table booking on your own site, with the times and table sizes you actually have.
  2. 02Deposits for larger groups, peak nights and set menus, with your cancellation rules shown before they pay.
  3. 03The menu as real text, not a PDF, with dietary and allergen information marked.
  4. 04A private dining and parties page with its own enquiry form.
  5. 05Opening hours, location and accessibility, kept the same as your Google Business Profile.

Questions

Restaurants and bars: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a restaurant?

Diners choose on the menu, the photos and the reviews, usually on a phone and often on the day. Groups and special occasions are booked further ahead, and those are the tables that cost most when nobody shows. The phone rings hardest during service, when nobody can answer it, and private hire enquiries end up in an inbox or a direct message that nobody gets back to.

Do I need my own booking page if I already use a booking platform?

It is worth having. A booking on your own site comes straight to you with the guest’s details, and you set the deposit rules. Whether you keep the platform as well is up to you.

Should the menu be a PDF?

No. A PDF is slow on a phone and hard for Google and AI assistants to read. A menu written as text loads fast, is easy to update and can show up when people search for a dish nearby.

How do deposits work for large groups?

You set the rule: which party sizes, which nights and how much. The booking page applies it, takes the deposit by card, and the deposit comes off the bill on the night.
