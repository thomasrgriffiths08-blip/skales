What it needs

What should a website for accountants do?

An accountant’s website needs a page for each service people search for, such as self assessment, limited company accounts, VAT, payroll and Making Tax Digital, and a clear statement of who you work with. It should let prospects book a discovery call straight into your diary, follow up fee proposals that go quiet, and chase clients for their records before each deadline.

  1. 01A page for each service: self assessment, limited company accounts, VAT returns, payroll, bookkeeping and Making Tax Digital.
  2. 02Who you work with, by type and by sector, such as sole traders, landlords, contractors or hospitality businesses.
  3. 03Your professional body and qualifications, and whether you are a chartered practice.
  4. 04How your fees work, in plain words, even if the figures come after a call.
  5. 05Discovery calls bookable online, at times that suit people who run businesses.

Questions

Accountants: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose an accountant?

New clients arrive at a moment of change: starting a business, going limited, taking on staff or falling behind with HMRC. They search for that problem, look for an accountant who understands businesses like theirs, and want to know how fees work before they speak to anyone. Work leaks through proposals that are never chased, discovery calls that take a string of emails to arrange, and records that turn up in the last days before the January deadline.

Can prospects book a discovery call straight into my diary?

Yes. They pick a free slot, say what they need help with, and get a confirmation and a reminder. The call lands in your diary and on the CRM board, ready for the proposal afterwards.

Can it chase clients for their records before the deadline?

Yes. Each client’s next deadline is saved, and ahead of it they get a text listing what you need from them. If nothing arrives, a second text goes out closer to the date.

Should my site say which kinds of client I work with?

Yes. A landlord or a contractor looking for an accountant wants to see that you understand their situation, and a page written for them does that far better than a general list of services.
