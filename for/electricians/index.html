What should a website for electricians do?

  • Your competent person scheme, such as NICEIC or NAPIT, with a link to where customers can check your registration.
  • A page for each job: fault finding, consumer unit upgrades, rewires, EV charge points and EICRs.
  • A landlord and letting agent page, with an enquiry form that takes more than one property at a time.
  • Photos of finished boards and tidy installs, because neat work is the one thing customers cannot judge any other way.
  • What the customer gets at the end of the job, certificates included, explained in plain words.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose an electrician?

Electrical work arrives two ways. Small faults come by phone from people who want someone this week, and they go with whoever answers and looks properly registered. Rewires, consumer units and EV chargers are researched first and priced against other firms, so those quotes go quiet unless someone follows them up, and landlord EICRs come round again and are easy to lose track of.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Electricians: questions people ask

Should my scheme logo go on my website?

Yes, with a link to where customers can check your registration. A logo on its own can be copied by anyone; a link to the register is proof.

Can letting agents send me lots of EICRs at once?

Yes. The landlord page takes several addresses in one enquiry, each property lands on the CRM board, and when each report is done its next due date is saved so the reminder goes out on its own.

Do I need a separate page for EV charger installs?

Yes. People search for the job rather than for an electrician, so a page that explains the survey, the install and what you need from them is the one that gets found. The same goes for consumer units and EICRs.
