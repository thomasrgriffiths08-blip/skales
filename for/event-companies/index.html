What should a website for event companies do?

  • An enquiry form that asks for the date, venue, guest numbers and type of event up front.
  • Photos and short films from real events, by type: weddings, corporate, parties and festivals.
  • What you provide and what you do not, so clients know which other suppliers they still need.
  • Separate pages for private and corporate clients, who search and decide differently.
  • The venues and the region you work across.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose an events company?

Event enquiries arrive months ahead, often from someone juggling several suppliers at once, so a clear quote that arrives first and then gets followed up stands out. Weddings are one-off, while corporate clients run the same party, launch or conference every year. Work leaks when quotes sit waiting on a budget sign-off, when dates are held on a promise rather than a deposit, and when last year’s corporate client is never contacted again.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Event companies: questions people ask

Can the enquiry form ask for the date, venue and guest numbers?

Yes, and it should. Those are the details you need to price the job or say you are already booked, so asking for them up front saves a round of emails.

Can it remind last year’s corporate clients?

Yes. When an annual event is done, next year’s date is saved, and the client gets a message well ahead of it asking whether they would like to book again.

What about enquiries for a date a year away?

They go on the board like any other, with the date attached, so they do not get lost between now and then. The quote is chased if it goes quiet, and the deposit holds the date once they decide.
