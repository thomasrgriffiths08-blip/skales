What it needs

What should a website for painters and decorators do?

A painter and decorator’s website needs photos of finished rooms and exteriors, the work you take on, from a single room to a whole house, and an enquiry form that asks for photos and sizes so you can price without a visit. It should then follow up every quote you send and ask for a review once the last coat is dry, because customers usually ask more than one decorator and the job tends to go to the one who replies first and keeps in touch.

  1. 01Photos of finished rooms, hallways and exteriors from your own jobs, sorted by the kind of work, with a line on what was done.
  2. 02The work you take on, from a single room or a front door to a whole house, wallpapering and exteriors.
  3. 03An enquiry form that asks for photos of the room, rough sizes and the finish wanted, so you can price without a visit.
  4. 04The areas you cover, and roughly how far ahead you are booked, kept up to date.
  5. 05Any trade body you belong to and your insurance, shown where a customer looks before letting you into their home.

Questions

Painters and decorators: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a decorator?

People start looking for a decorator when they have just moved in, are about to sell, or have finally had enough of a room. They ask two or three firms for a price, often through a form or a message sent in the evening, and then wait. While you are up a ladder the messages pile up, and a quote you priced on Sunday night can sit unanswered for a week because nobody followed it up. Exterior work bunches into the dry months, so the diary can be packed in summer and thin in winter.

Can I price a decorating job from photos?

Often, yes. The enquiry form asks for photos of each room, rough sizes and the finish the customer wants, so you can send a price or a range straight away and only visit for the bigger jobs.

How do I follow up quotes without being pushy?

Let the system do it. A short, friendly message goes a few days after the quote, and another later if there is no answer, and both stop the moment the customer replies or accepts.

Do I need a website if most of my work is word of mouth?

Word of mouth still ends with a search. The person recommended to you looks you up before they get in touch, and a page with photos of your finished work and a quick way to ask for a price turns that recommendation into an enquiry.
