What it needs

What should a website for tattoo studios do?

A tattoo studio website needs each artist’s portfolio on its own page, and an enquiry form that takes the idea, placement, size and reference images in one go. It should then book consultations and sittings online with a deposit that holds the date, so a no-show costs the customer rather than the artist.

  1. 01A portfolio page for each artist, sorted by style, with healed photos as well as fresh ones.
  2. 02One enquiry form that asks for the idea, placement, size, reference images and the artist they want.
  3. 03Consultations and sittings booked online, with a deposit taken to hold the date.
  4. 04Your deposit, cancellation and rescheduling policy, written plainly before anyone pays.
  5. 05An age check in the booking, asking for date of birth and reminding customers to bring photo ID.

Questions

Tattoo studios: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a tattoo studio?

People find a tattooist through the work: they scroll an artist’s portfolio, settle on a style, then send a message with an idea and a photo. Those messages arrive across three apps, half of them never say where or how big, and the back-and-forth eats the evenings. Once a date is set, a no-show leaves an artist with an empty chair and a design drawn for nobody.

Should a tattoo studio take deposits online?

Yes, for anything booked in advance. The customer pays when they pick the date, your policy is shown before they pay, and the deposit can come off the price on the day. It stops dates being held by people who never turn up.

Can enquiries from Instagram and the website land in one place?

Yes. The link in your bio and the website both open the same enquiry form, so every request arrives with the idea, size, placement and photos attached, and sits on the CRM board until it is booked or turned down.

How should a tattoo portfolio show healed work?

Put a fresh and a healed photo of each piece side by side on the artist’s page. Healed work is what a careful customer looks for, and it is proof a photo taken straight after the session cannot give.
