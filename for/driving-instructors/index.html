What it needsDriving instructors

What should a website for driving instructors do?

  • That you are a DVSA approved driving instructor, with your ADI number, so learners know you are fully qualified rather than a trainee.
  • The areas you cover and where you pick up from: home, school, college or work.
  • Manual or automatic, and what else you teach, such as intensive courses, refresher lessons and motorway lessons.
  • Lessons and blocks booked and paid for online, with your cancellation policy shown first.
  • A waiting list form for when you are full, asking for the area, the gearbox and any test date.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a driving instructor?

Learners, and often their parents, search for an instructor nearby, check manual or automatic, and ring or message a few. You cannot pick up during a lesson, so enquiries pile up until the evening and some have booked elsewhere by then. Once a pupil starts, the leaks are lessons cancelled on the day, blocks that run out with no new one booked, and a waiting list kept in your head.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Driving instructors: questions people ask

Can pupils book and pay for driving lessons online?

Yes. They pick a slot from the times you open, pay for a single lesson or a block, and get a confirmation and a reminder. When a block is nearly used up, the pupil is reminded to book the next one.

Should I show my ADI number on my website?

Yes. Learners and parents want to know they are paying a fully qualified instructor, and your ADI number and DVSA approval on the page answer that before they ask.

Can I keep a waiting list on my website?

Yes. When you are full, the booking page becomes a waiting list form, and each name lands on the CRM board with their area, gearbox and test date, so the right pupil is offered a slot when one opens.
