What it needsBuilders and joiners

What should a website for builders and joiners do?

  • Project pages with photos from strip-out to handover, so people see how you work as well as the result.
  • A clear line on what you take on, from extensions and renovations to bespoke joinery, so the wrong enquiries stay away.
  • An enquiry form that asks what the project is, whether there are drawings yet and when they hope to start.
  • How you work with architects and building control, explained once in plain words.
  • The trade bodies you belong to and your insurance, where a cautious customer will look for them.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a builder?

Building work mostly starts with a recommendation, and the first thing that person does is look you up. They want to see jobs like theirs, in houses like theirs, and some sign that you turn up and finish. Then they gather quotes and take their time, so work leaks in the gap between the site visit and the decision, and evenings disappear on enquiries for jobs you would never take.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Builders and joiners: questions people ask

My work comes from word of mouth. Do I need a website?

The person you were recommended to will still look you up before they ring. The website is where that recommendation turns into a call, with photos of the kind of job they heard about.

How do I stop enquiries for jobs I don’t do?

Say plainly on the site what you take on and where, and ask the right questions on the form. You can then see at a glance which enquiries fit before you ring anyone back.

Can I send quotes from the system?

Yes. You price the job and send it from the CRM, and the customer gets a link to read and accept it. If it goes quiet a follow-up goes out, and accepted quotes move to Booked.
