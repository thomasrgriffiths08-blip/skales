What it needs

What should a website for dental practices do?

A dental practice website needs to answer the first question straight away: whether you are taking on new NHS or private patients, and how to register. It also needs online booking for check-ups and hygiene appointments, each clinician listed with their GDC registration, clear help for dental emergencies, and recall reminders that go out on their own.

  1. 01Whether you are taking new NHS patients, private patients or both, on the home page and kept up to date.
  2. 02Online booking for check-ups, hygiene and new patient appointments, by clinician.
  3. 03Each clinician’s qualifications and GDC registration number.
  4. 04What to do in a dental emergency, in and out of hours.
  5. 05Private treatment pages that explain what each treatment involves and how fees work.

Questions

Dental practices: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a dental practice?

People looking for a dentist want to know one thing first: whether you will take them on, and on what basis. Existing patients want to book check-ups and hygiene appointments without phoning the moment the line opens, and urgent cases want to know whether there is a same-day slot. Recalls are where work leaks: patients mean to book their next check-up, forget, and drift until something hurts.

Should the website say if we are not taking NHS patients?

Yes. It is the first thing people want to know, and saying it clearly spares your reception the same call all day. If you are taking private patients, say how to register.

Can check-up recalls go by text?

Yes. Each patient’s next check-up date is saved when they are seen, and a recall text goes out before it so they can pick a time online. Anyone who does not book gets one reminder closer to the date.

Can patients book with a specific dentist or hygienist?

Yes. They choose the appointment type and the clinician, then a free time. Taken slots disappear, so nobody is double booked.
