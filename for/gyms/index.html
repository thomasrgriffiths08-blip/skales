What should a website for gyms and fitness studios do?

  • The class timetable on the page itself, not a PDF or a photo on social media.
  • Free trials, inductions and personal training bookable online.
  • Membership options and what each one includes, in plain words.
  • Real photos of the space, the kit and the classes, taken at the times people actually train.
  • Coach and trainer profiles, so people can choose who they train with.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a gym?

People choose a gym by what is close, what the classes are and what it feels like inside, so they check the timetable and the photos before they visit. They want to try before they commit, and the free trial is where they are won or lost. Work leaks when the front desk is empty during a class, when trial sessions are booked and not turned up to, and when personal training enquiries sit unread in direct messages.

Gyms and fitness studios: questions people ask

Can people book a free trial online?

Yes. They pick a trial or induction slot from the times you open, leave their details, and get a confirmation and a reminder. It lands in your diary like any other booking.

Can the website take membership payments?

The booking system takes deposits on sessions such as personal training. Recurring memberships usually stay in the membership software you already use, and the site links to it clearly.

Should the timetable be on the website or on social media?

On the website, as real text. Posts disappear down the feed, while a timetable on your site can be found by Google and read by AI assistants answering what classes are on nearby.
