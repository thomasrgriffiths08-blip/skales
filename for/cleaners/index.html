What should a website for cleaning companies do?

A cleaning company’s website has to earn trust before anyone hands over a key: who your cleaners are, how they are vetted and insured, and how keys are kept. It should let customers book one-off and end-of-tenancy cleans online with a deposit, and turn regular and office cleaning enquiries into quotes that get followed up.

  1. 01How your cleaners are vetted, including DBS checks if you do them, and your insurance.
  2. 02How keys and alarm codes are handled, in plain words.
  3. 03Separate pages for regular, deep, end-of-tenancy and office cleaning, each with what is included.
  4. 04One-off cleans bookable online with a deposit; regular and office cleaning by quote.
  5. 05The postcodes you cover, checked before the customer fills anything in.

Questions

Cleaning companies: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a cleaner?

Choosing a cleaner means letting a stranger into your home, so people read the website for signs of care: vetting, insurance, the same cleaner each time. End-of-tenancy and deep cleans are booked against fixed dates and decided quickly, while regular domestic and office cleaning starts with a quote. Work leaks through evening enquiries nobody answers, one-off customers never offered a regular clean, and cancellations at the door.

Should I take a deposit for end-of-tenancy cleans?

It suits them well, because the date is fixed around a move and a late cancellation leaves a gap you cannot fill. You choose which cleans take a deposit and how much, and the booking page shows your cancellation rules before anyone pays.

How do I show customers they can trust my cleaners?

Say how you vet and insure your team, how keys are handled and whether customers get the same cleaner each time. Real photos of your team do more than any badge.

Can it handle office cleaning enquiries as well as homes?

Yes. Office enquiries go to a quote form instead of the booking page, land on the CRM board, and the quote is followed up if it goes quiet.
