What it needsNail technicians and nail salons

What should a website for nail technicians and nail salons do?

  • Every treatment with its length: gel manicures, builder gel, acrylic and gel extensions, infills, removals, pedicures and nail art.
  • Online booking that only offers slots long enough for the treatment chosen, so a full set never lands in a removal slot.
  • Photos of your own work, sorted by style, with a booking button under them.
  • A first-visit form for allergies and nail health, filled in before the appointment.
  • Where you work, whether a salon, a room at home or mobile, and your cancellation policy in plain words.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a nail technician?

Most clients find a nail technician through photos, on Instagram or on a friend’s hands, and then they message to ask about prices and times. Those messages arrive while you are mid-set and cannot reply, and by the time you do they have booked with someone who has a link. Once a client is with you the work is regular, but it leaks through infills nobody rebooked, cancellations the night before and the client who quietly stops coming.

Nail technicians and nail salons: questions people ask

Can clients book from my Instagram?

Yes. Your booking link goes in your bio and in your replies, and it opens your own booking page with your treatments, your times and your deposit, so someone who finds you through a photo can book without waiting for you to reply.

Should a nail technician take a deposit?

For the longer treatments it is worth it. The client pays when they book, sees your cancellation policy before they do, and a no-show is covered by the rules you set instead of costing you the slot.

Can a cancelled slot be filled automatically?

Yes. Clients who want to come in sooner go on a waiting list, and when an appointment is cancelled the list gets a text, so the first to reply takes the slot without you messaging round.
