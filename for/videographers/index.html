What it needsFilm and photography

What should a website for film and photography do?

  • A showreel and galleries that load only when someone presses play or scrolls to them, so the page stays fast on a phone.
  • An enquiry form that asks for the date, venue and type of shoot, so you can check availability first.
  • Packages and what each includes, explained plainly, even if the price comes after a call.
  • Separate pages for weddings, corporate film, events and portraits, which are different searches.
  • If you fly a drone, your CAA operator ID and the insurance that covers it.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a videographer?

Couples and businesses shortlist on the work, and then the first question is whether you are free on their date. Wedding clients book a long way ahead and compare several shooters, while corporate clients want a proposal quickly. Work leaks when a date is pencilled in but never secured, when a quote goes out and nobody follows it up, and when a heavy portfolio takes so long to load on a phone that people leave before the first frame.

Straight answersThe questions people ask

Film and photography: questions people ask

Will a showreel slow my site down?

Not if it is built properly. The video loads only when someone presses play, with a still frame in its place until then, so the page is quick even on a weak signal.

Can I take a booking fee to hold a date?

Yes. Once you have agreed the job, the client pays the booking fee online, the date is marked as theirs, and the fee comes off the final invoice.

How do I handle enquiries for dates I already have booked?

The enquiry form asks for the date first, so you can see at a glance which enquiries you can take. You can then reply to the rest straight away, which people remember.
