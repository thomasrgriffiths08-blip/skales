What should a website for cafés do?

A café website needs your opening hours, menu, location and the small things people check before they visit, such as dogs, laptops and step-free access, all written as text that matches your Google Business Profile. Where you take afternoon tea bookings, celebration cakes or catering orders, it should book them with a deposit and follow up the bigger quotes.

  1. 01Opening hours on every page, kept the same as Google, including bank holidays and seasonal changes.
  2. 02The menu as real text, with vegan, gluten-free and other dietary options marked.
  3. 03The practical details people check: dogs, laptops, high chairs, parking and step-free access.
  4. 04Afternoon tea or brunch bookings, and cake orders with a collection slot, taken online with a deposit.
  5. 05A catering and private hire page with its own enquiry form.

Questions

Cafés: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a café?

People find a café on a map, then tap through to check you are open, what is on the menu and whether they can bring the dog or the laptop. Wrong opening hours lose them on the spot. The bookable work, afternoon teas, cake orders, office catering and private hire, comes in by phone and message during the lunch rush, and that is exactly when it gets lost.

Does a café really need a website?

If people find you on Google Maps, the website is where they check the menu, whether you take dogs and whether you are open. It also gives Google and AI assistants the facts they need to answer those questions about you.

Can customers order cakes online?

Yes. They choose the cake and a collection slot and pay a deposit, and the order lands in your diary with their notes. Larger or custom orders can come in as an enquiry for you to price.

Can I take bookings for afternoon tea but keep walk-ins for everything else?

Yes. The booking page only shows the slots you choose to open, so afternoon tea can be booked while the rest of the café stays walk-in.
