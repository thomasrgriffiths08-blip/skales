What should a website for home care agencies do?

A home care agency’s website has two jobs: helping families choose care and helping you recruit carers. In England your latest CQC rating must be on show once you have been rated. Families should be able to book an assessment visit online and read plainly about each kind of care you give, and carers should be able to apply in a few minutes on a phone.

  1. 01Your CQC rating shown as the rules require, or your Care Inspectorate, Care Inspectorate Wales or RQIA report outside England.
  2. 02Each kind of care you give, such as personal care, companionship, dementia care and live in care, in plain words.
  3. 03Assessment visits bookable online, with a form a relative can fill in on someone’s behalf.
  4. 04The areas you cover, so families know straight away whether you can help.
  5. 05A careers page with an application that works on a phone, because that is where carers apply.

Home care agencies: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a home care agency?

Families usually look for home care after a hospital stay, a fall or a slow change they can no longer manage, and the person searching is often a son or daughter who lives elsewhere. They check the CQC rating, compare a few agencies and want to talk to someone soon. The leaks are calls missed out of hours, enquiries that wait days for an assessment, and carers who give up on a long application form.

Do I have to show my CQC rating on my website?

In England, yes. Once you have been rated, the rules say your latest CQC rating must be shown on your website and at your premises, so put it where families will see it.

Can a relative book an assessment for someone else?

Yes. The form asks who is booking and who the care is for, so a son or daughter can arrange the visit and be kept in the loop.

Can my website help me recruit carers?

Yes. A careers page with a short application that works on a phone gets more carers to apply, and each application lands on your board to follow up.
