What should a website for plumbers and heating engineers do?

  • Your Gas Safe registration number on every page, with a link so customers can check it on the Gas Safe Register.
  • A call button that stays on screen on a phone, for the person standing next to a cold radiator.
  • Separate pages for breakdowns, annual servicing, new boilers and landlord gas safety certificates.
  • Annual services bookable online, so planned work fills the diary without phone tag.
  • The towns and postcodes you cover, so nobody rings from outside your patch.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a heating engineer?

Nobody shops around for a boiler repair on a cold evening. They search on their phone, ring the first number that looks local and Gas Safe, and if it rings out they ring the next one. Servicing is the opposite: steady yearly work that drifts away because nobody reminds the customer, while new boiler quotes go quiet as the customer waits on two more.

Plumbers and heating engineers: questions people ask

Should my Gas Safe number be on my website?

Yes, on every page, with a link to the Gas Safe Register so a customer can check it in seconds. It is one of the first things people look for before they let someone work on their boiler.

Can it remind landlords when their gas safety certificate is due?

Yes. The date of each check is saved against the property, and the landlord gets a text before the next one is due with a link to book. A landlord with several properties has a date for each.

I’m booked up already. Why would I want online booking?

Because it lets you decide what goes in the diary. Services are booked online into the slots you open, while breakdowns still come to you by phone, so the planned work stops interrupting the urgent work.
