What should a website for solicitors do?

  • The SRA digital badge, and the price and service information the Transparency Rules require for the services they cover.
  • Practice area pages written for the client’s situation: buying a house, separating, losing a job, dealing with an estate.
  • A consultation booking that asks for the other party’s name, so you can run a conflict check before the call.
  • Who each solicitor is, what they specialise in and how to reach them.
  • Your complaints procedure, easy to find.
Your customersHow the work comes in

How do customers choose a solicitor?

People looking for a solicitor are usually in the middle of something: a house move, a separation, a dispute at work, a death in the family. Conveyancing is shopped around on price and speed, while family and employment clients choose on whether you seem to understand them, and often search late at night. Work leaks through conveyancing quotes that are never followed up, enquiries left until morning, and consultations that take several emails to arrange.

Solicitors: questions people ask

Does our website need the SRA badge?

If you are regulated by the SRA, yes. The Transparency Rules require the SRA digital badge on your website, along with price and service information for certain services and details of how to complain, and your compliance officer signs off the wording before anything goes live.

Is a missed-call text appropriate for a law firm?

Yes, if the wording is right. The message is written with you, says only that you missed their call and how to book or reply, and never mentions the matter.

Will chasing conveyancing quotes look pushy?

Not one polite follow-up a couple of days after the quote. It reads as a firm that is organised and wants the work, and it only goes to quotes that are still open.
