What it needs

What should a website for plasterers do?

A plasterer’s website needs photos of finished walls and ceilings, the work you take on, from patching and skimming to rendering and dry lining, and an enquiry form that asks for photos and rough sizes so you can price without a visit. It should then follow up every quote and show when you are next free, because plastering usually sits between other trades and the job goes to whoever replies first and gives a date.

  1. 01Photos of finished walls, ceilings and render from your own jobs, with a line on what each was: a reskim, a patch after a rewire, a whole house.
  2. 02The work you take on: patching, skimming, boarding and dry lining, rendering and coving, and whether you take small jobs.
  3. 03An enquiry form that asks for photos of the wall or ceiling, rough sizes and the date it needs to be ready for the next trade.
  4. 04How far ahead you are booked, kept current, so customers can plan the decorator or fitter around you.
  5. 05The areas you cover and your insurance, shown where a homeowner checks before you start.

Questions

Plasterers: questions people ask

More in every question, answered.

How do customers choose a plasterer?

Most people need a plasterer at a fixed point in a bigger job: after a rewire, before a new kitchen, after a damp repair, or when an old ceiling finally cracks. They ask the builder or electrician who just left, and if that fails they search and message two or three plasterers at once. Timing matters more than in most trades, because the decorator or fitter is booked to follow, and a plasterer who cannot give a date loses the work. While you are on a wall that has to be finished before it goes off, the phone rings out and messages wait until evening.

Can a plasterer price a job from photos?

For most patching, skimming and ceilings, yes. The form asks for photos and rough sizes, so you can send a price or a range quickly and only visit for the larger or awkward jobs.

How do I stop losing enquiries while I am on the tools?

Missed calls get a text straight back with a link to send photos, and every enquiry lands in one list with its photos attached, so you can price them together at the end of the day.

Should my website show how far ahead I am booked?

Yes. Customers are usually fitting you in between other trades, so a current line on your next free dates saves calls and brings the enquiries that fit your diary.
