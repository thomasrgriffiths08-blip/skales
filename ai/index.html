    How it is set up

    Built on your business, not a script

    Before it takes a call it is given your diary, your price list and the rules you work by. That is how it can book the job, and how it knows when to stop.

    • Your diary.It offers only the times that are really free, and the booking goes straight in.
    • Your prices.It quotes from your price list. If a job is not on it, it says so and does not guess.
    • Your rules.The safety question you always ask, your deposit, your cancellation terms. It follows them on every call.
    • You, when it matters.Anything urgent or unusual goes to your phone with the details. Every call leaves you a summary.

    Where it stops

    Three things it will never do

    1. 01Pretend to be a personIt says it is an AI in its first sentence, on every call. Listen for it in the three calls above.
    2. 02Make up a priceIn the garage call it is asked what a brake repair will cost. It will not guess, and offers a call back from the mechanic.
    3. 03Skip your rulesDan’s rule is to ask about a gas smell before anything else. Listen to the heating call: it asks that before it talks about times or prices.

    Straight answers

    What people ask first

    Are the calls on this page real?

    No. They are recorded examples: scripts written for made-up businesses, and every voice is AI, the callers too. They let you hear how a call goes and what the assistant does during it. An agent for your business is set up on your own diary, price list and rules.

    Will my callers know it is an AI?

    Yes. It says so in its first sentence. Nobody is tricked into thinking they are talking to a person.

    What happens when it cannot answer something?

    It says so, takes the details, tells the caller who will ring them back, and sends you the note. It does not guess at prices or advice.

    Does it replace the person who answers my phone?

    It covers the calls nobody can take: when you are on a job, with a customer, or closed for the night. Anything it should not handle is passed to a person with the details.

    What does an AI phone agent cost?

    A flat price for the build, agreed on a call before anything starts. Book a call and you will get a straight answer for your business.

    Sam Whitfield calls Larchfield Heating

    Dan clocked off at 17:30. The phone rings at 23:10.

    AI assistant: Larchfield Heating, this is Dan’s AI assistant. He’s off for the night, but I can sort most things. What’s happened?

    Sam Whitfield: Oh. Hiya, sorry, I know it’s late. Erm, the boiler’s just gone off. There’s a code on the front, F28? And we’ve got no hot water at all.

    AI assistant: Oh no, not what you want at this time of night. Before anything else, can you smell gas anywhere in the house?

    Sam Whitfield: No. No, nothing like that.

    AI assistant: Okay, good. And is anything leaking under the boiler, or is it dry?

    Sam Whitfield: Er, hang on, let me look. No, it’s dry. There’s a little dial on it that says one and a half? If that means anything.

    AI assistant: It does, that’s the pressure, so Dan knows what to bring. He can come first thing tomorrow, at eight. A breakdown visit is from £95. Shall I book that in?

    Sam Whitfield: Oh, yes please. Yeah, eight’s fine. It’s Sam, Sam Whitfield.

    AI assistant: Thanks, Sam. And what’s the address?

    Sam Whitfield: 12 Ash Grove, LS6, er, 1RT.

    AI assistant: So that’s 12 Ash Grove, LS6 1RT, tomorrow at eight. Is this mobile the best number to text?

    Sam Whitfield: Yeah, that’s me.

    AI assistant: Perfect, you’re booked in. I’ve texted you a link for a £30 deposit, which comes off the bill, and Dan will text you when he’s on his way.

    Sam Whitfield: Brilliant. Honestly, thank you. I thought we’d be boiling kettles all week.

    AI assistant: No problem at all. Night, Sam.

    Sam Whitfield: Cheers. Night.

    A job booked at 23:12 while Dan slept.

    Owen Hughes calls Kerb & Camber

    Both mechanics are under a car. The office phone rings.

    AI assistant: Kerb and Camber, this is the garage’s AI assistant. Both mechanics are under a car, so I’m on the phones. What can I do for you?

    Owen Hughes: Oh, right. Hi, yeah. Erm, my MOT runs out next week, and I completely forgot about it. Have you got anything before Friday?

    AI assistant: Let me have a look. I’ve got Wednesday at nine, or Thursday at half one. An MOT is £54.85.

    Owen Hughes: Wednesday at nine, yeah. Do that. Oh, and sorry, while I’ve got you, there’s a sort of knocking when I brake? Like a clunk. Roughly what’s that going to cost?

    AI assistant: Honestly, I can’t price that until someone’s had a look, and I’d rather not guess and get it wrong. I can add a brake check to Wednesday, and get Baz to ring you this afternoon. Would that help?

    Owen Hughes: Yeah, no, that’s fair. Go on then.

    AI assistant: Right. MOT on Wednesday at nine, brake check added, and Baz will call you today. Can I take your name?

    Owen Hughes: Owen. Owen Hughes.

    AI assistant: Thanks, Owen. And what’s the car?

    Owen Hughes: It’s a Corsa. Silver Corsa, 2016, I think. Yeah, 2016.

    AI assistant: Lovely. I’ve texted you the booking with our address. See you Wednesday.

    Owen Hughes: Cheers. Bye.

    The MOT booked, and the hard question passed to a person.

    Hannah Price calls Fern Physio

    Both physios are with patients. Nobody is at the desk.

    AI assistant: Fern Physio, you’re through to the clinic’s AI assistant. Both physios are with patients at the minute. How can I help?

    Hannah Price: Oh, hiya. It’s Hannah, Hannah Price. Erm, I’m booked in on Thursday, but they’ve just put me on a late shift, so I can’t actually make it now.

    AI assistant: Ah, that’s annoying. Let me find you. Yes, here you are, Thursday at half four with Dev. Do you want to move it?

    Hannah Price: Yeah, if I can. Is there anything on Friday?

    AI assistant: Dev’s got quarter past ten, or two o’clock.

    Hannah Price: Ooh, ten’s, no, wait, I’m at work. Two. Two would be perfect.

    AI assistant: Done. Friday at two with Dev. I’ve texted you the new time, and Thursday’s slot is free for someone else now.

    Hannah Price: Oh, amazing. Erm, sorry, do I need to bring anything? It’s my knee, so should I wear shorts, or...?

    AI assistant: Shorts are a good idea, or anything loose you can roll up. Dev will want to see the knee moving.

    Hannah Price: Perfect. Thank you so much.

    AI assistant: You’re welcome, Hannah. See you Friday.

    Hannah Price: Ta-ra.
