Overhead

Scroll to climb — the valley draws itself in contours as you rise. The page is an ascent. Scrolling is altitude: the telemetry counts up, a fictional Peak District dale inks itself in from the valley floor to the tops, and by 120 m the whole view has tilted from an oblique into a flat survey sheet. The map is generated in the browser from one height field, so the contours, the dry-stone walls, the hedgerows and the brook all come out of the same arithmetic.

What does Overhead do?

Overhead is a working website built for an invented drone survey & filming. Drone operators all show the same thing — a gallery of pretty aerial photographs. What they are actually paid for is measurement, so this page is built out of a map that measures itself, and the roof survey is shown twice: once as a picture, once as something you can put a tape to.

It sits in the Motion lane: the page is the film: scroll-driven sequences, letterboxes, timecodes, drawn frames.

What is in it

  • Scroll is altitude — the contour map draws itself in as the aircraft climbs
  • Telemetry pinned at the top: altitude, a moving heading tape, wind and battery
  • At the top the view tilts from oblique through to a full plan-view sheet
  • Services as a real flight log; hover a job and its contour band lights up on the keymap
  • A picker rewrites the log for a roof, a site, land or a property to sell
  • Before and after roof: the frame as flown against a measurable orthomosaic, with a draggable divider
  • Enquiry form takes a postcode and returns a draft flight plan

Written

None at runtime. Fonts load from Google Fonts with system fallbacks; everything else — every drawing, chart and interaction — is in the file.

No stock. Every visual is drawn in code, SVG or canvas.

Runs on
Any modern browser, and it is built for phones as much as desktops. State stays in your browser; there is a reset control inside.
