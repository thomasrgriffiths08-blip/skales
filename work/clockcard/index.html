Clockcard

Staff clock in from the van; the office watches the week fill up. Two views of one clock. On a phone the fitter picks the job, taps clock in and gets a running timer, a break button and a note for the office. On the desk it is a week grid of people against days with overtime shaded, every job's hours set against the estimate, and a week that can be approved and exported.

What it provesYou find out a job is running over while it is still running, not when the invoice goes out.

What does Clockcard do?

Clockcard is a working tool built to work for any service business. Most small firms find out a job ran over when they add up the invoice. This puts the clock in the fitter's pocket and the running total on the owner's screen at the same moment.

It sits in the Broad lane: works for any service business: booking, quotes, the pipeline, follow-up, reviews.

What is in it

  • Clock in, switch job, take a break, clock out — the timer runs against the real clock
  • Owner's week grid: people against days, overtime shaded, click a cell to correct an entry
  • Hours booked against the estimate for every job, over and under said in plain words
  • Approve or send a week back per person, then copy the payroll CSV out of a textarea
  • House rules — standard day, overtime thresholds, unpaid break, rounding — recalculate everything above
  • Clocking in on the phone shows up in the owner's grid and the job bars immediately
