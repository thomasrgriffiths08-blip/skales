The workshop

We build the boxes. That is the whole trick.

Every carcass that leaves Bellingham Yard is cut on our own beam saw from 18mm moisture-resistant board, dowelled and glued, not screwed through a cam. It is the least glamorous sentence on this website and it is the reason our doors still shut squarely in year twelve.

We are eleven people. Four in the workshop, four on the tools, two in the drawing office and Marguerite, who runs everything and will tell you plainly if what you have asked for is a bad idea. We fit around fifty kitchens a year and we would rather do forty-eight of them properly.

Because we make the cabinetry ourselves, we can price it honestly, in public, on a web page — which is what the engine below does. It uses the same rate card the drawing office uses on a Monday morning.

The drawing updates as you choose. Layout affects installation time rather than materials — a U-shape has twice the corners to scribe, and corners are where the hours go.

How many units?

Base units sit on the floor and carry the worktop; wall units hang above. Most family kitchens land between ten and fourteen base units. Each base unit is 600mm wide.
