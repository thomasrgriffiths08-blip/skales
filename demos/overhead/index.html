A ladder shows you one slope at a time

A drone shows you the whole roof in eleven minutes, from four metres above the ridge, at a resolution where you can count the slipped slates.

Overhead is two of us and a van: a licensed remote pilot and a surveyor who processes what comes back. We fly roofs for roofers, progress for site managers, land for agents and farms, and film for anyone who needs their building to look like it is worth the money.

Everything comes back as files you can actually use — a marked-up defect map, an orthomosaic you can measure off, a flight log with the times and the weather on it. Not a folder of photographs for you to sort out.

  • HeightWe fly to 120 m and no higher without written permission. Most roof work happens between 20 and 40 m.
  • WeatherWe do not launch above 24 kt at the ridge, in rain, or when the cloud base is below the site. If the day turns, we move it and you are not charged.
  • PaperworkLicensed operator, £5m public liability. The permission, the risk assessment and the flight log come with the survey, not after you ask.
  • TurnaroundStills the same evening. Orthomosaic and defect map inside two working days.
