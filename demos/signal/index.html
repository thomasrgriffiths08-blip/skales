Every figure on this site is a real number from our own production file. If we do not know it, we do not print it.

SIGNAL is not a music festival. It is a three-day argument about what sound can do to a room, and what a room does back.

We book people who build their own instruments. Modular rigs held together with gaffer and stubbornness. Choirs routed through guitar pedals. A woman who plays a decommissioned rail signal box. If it arrives in a flight case with a laptop and a preset, it is not for us.

We do not do VIP, brand activations, immersive experiences. We do doors, a decent PA, and a printed timetable that tells the truth.

Everything is labelled. Stages have numbers. Sets have times. The programme lists the fee band each act was paid, because secrecy about money only ever protects the person holding it.

Three of the four rooms are under 400 capacity on purpose. You should be able to see the hands doing the work. The fourth is a 1910 goods hall with a 400-box array and a nine-second reverb tail, and on the Sunday we point all of it at one chord for eleven minutes.

Come alone if you like. Nobody here is performing at you.

— SIGNAL programming floor, Manchester, February 2026
