Atelier Voss

We build slowly, in three materials, for people who intend to stay.

Eleven people working from a grain loft on the Ostrand quay. Forty-one completed buildings, none of which we have ever photographed. What follows is drawn.

Selected Works

02  Position Ostrand, 2026 Sheet A-1.01

A building
outlives the
argument that
made it.

We work in concrete, timber, and the light that falls between them. Three materials is not a limitation we chose for elegance — it is what a practice of eleven people can honestly claim to understand.

Every project begins the same way: a week on site doing nothing. We measure where the light lands at seven in the morning and where it has gone by five. We note which corner the wind finds. We watch how people already cross the ground before we give them a door. Only then does anyone draw.

Concrete is poured once. That single fact governs the practice. We cast every mix three times in the yard behind the loft, leave the panels through a winter, and choose from what the weather returns. Timber gets the same patience — larch that will silver, oak that will not, and no stain on either.

We do not photograph our buildings. Photographs flatten a room into one moment at one hour under one sky, and we have never made a room that was about one hour. So the practice publishes drawings instead: plans, sections, details, the same sheets the builders worked from. If you want to know what it is like inside, come to Ostrand and we will take you.

A wall is not a surface. It is a decision about who is inside. Marit Voss — inaugural lecture, Ostrand School of Building, 2019

04  One building, three sheets Halvard House · Ostrand Fjord Sheets A-121 · A-301 · A-541
Each sheet drags sideways
SHEET 01 / 03 — PLAN

The plan is an argument about walking.

You enter at the narrow end, against the hill, and the house widens as you move through it. The stair is placed in the middle of the depth rather than against a wall, so that nobody arrives anywhere without first passing the light coming down it.

SHEET 02 / 03 — SECTION BB

The section is where the light is decided.

Cut through the stair and the house explains itself: a 250 mm concrete shell holding a timber box off the ground, and a rooflight the width of the landing that drops a single moving band of sun down the north wall from March to October.

SHEET 03 / 03 — DETAIL 07

And the detail is where it is paid for.

The parapet is the whole building in miniature: concrete stopped 40 mm proud of the timber so water is thrown clear, a shadow gap that reads as a black line from the quay, and a fall of 1:60 that nobody will ever see but the roof will remember.

05  How the work is made Four stages · no shortcuts Sheet A-4.01
01

Standing

One week on site before a single line is drawn. We go at 07:00 and again at 17:00, in whatever weather is offered, and we write down what we find: where the ground already drains, which corner the wind finds, the line people have worn crossing the plot without being told to. We photograph nothing. We sketch and we measure. By the end of the week we can usually say where the building should not go, which is the more useful half of the answer.

Deliverable — site notebook, hand survey, one A1 sheet

02

Drawing

Everything is drawn by hand at 1:50 before anything is modelled. A pencil is slower than a mouse and that is the entire point — you cannot copy-paste a wall you had to draw. When the plan feels settled we tape it out at full size on the model-shop floor and walk it. Roughly a third of our plans change after that walk, usually because a corridor that read as generous on paper turns out to be a corridor.

Deliverable — 1:50 hand set, 1:1 tape-out, sketch model at 1:100

03

Making

Samples before specification, always. Every concrete mix is cast three times in the yard behind the loft and left through a winter; we choose from what the weather returns rather than from what the batching plant promises. Timber is bought standing where we can and air-dried where we must. We attend the first pour on every project — all eleven of us, at six in the morning, which is a superstition we have declined to give up.

Deliverable — sample wall, mix schedule, 1:5 details for every junction

04

Keeping

We return at one year, five years and ten. We photograph the joints — the only photographs we take — measure the movement, and write the building a short report which goes to the owner and into the archive. Twelve buildings have now been through the ten-year visit. Four of them taught us to stop doing something. That is what the visits are for.

Deliverable — condition report at Y1, Y5, Y10 · held in the practice archive
