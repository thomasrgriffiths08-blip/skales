    A website is only worth the jobs it brings in.

    The draft is the easy part. This is how the real one gets found in your town, and what happens when someone lands on it.

    How you get found

    1. A page for every service, in your town.People search for the job and the place, not your name. Each service gets its own page, written for where you work.
      • hartleyheating.co.uk
      • /boiler-repair-leeds
      • /boiler-service-leeds
      • /new-boiler-leeds
      • /radiator-repair-leeds
    2. Your Google Business Profile, matched to the site.The same name, number, hours and services in both places, so Google trusts them. That is what gets you into the map results.
    3. Reviews that ask for themselves.After every job the customer gets a text with a link to review you. More reviews, and recent ones, keep you near the top of the map.
    4. Written so AI answers can quote it.Plain answers to what your customers ask, with the structured data search engines read, so ChatGPT and Google’s AI answers can name you.
    5. Ads for when you want work this week.Google and Meta ads pointed at the booking page, and tracked to the booked job, not to clicks.

    How visits turn into booked jobs

    1. Booking with a deposit.Real slots from your diary, any hour. A deposit holds the slot, so nobody books and vanishes.
    2. Missed calls get a text back.On a job when they ring? They get a text within a minute with a link to book, before they ring the next name on Google.
    3. Quotes chase themselves.A polite nudge goes out if a quote has not been answered, so fewer of them go cold.
    4. One place for every enquiry.Calls, texts, forms and bookings in one list on your phone, each with where it came from.

    On the call, I have your draft open.

    1. Your draft, and what people in your town type into Google when they need you.
    2. Who is getting those jobs now, and what they are doing that you are not.
    3. A flat price and a date, if it is a fit. If it is not, you will hear that too.
